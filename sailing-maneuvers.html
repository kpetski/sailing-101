Tiller Quick Reference
Head Up Push the tiller away from you (toward leeward/the sail) → bow turns toward the wind.
Fall Off Pull the tiller in toward you (toward windward) → bow turns away from the wind.
This assumes you're sitting on the windward (higher, wind-hitting) side — the usual spot. It's the same push-opposite/pull-same rule you already know from steering: tiller motion is always opposite the direction you want the bow to go. Heading up and falling off are just small versions of that. A tack is the same push (away from you, toward the sail) as heading up — just held hard over, all the way through the wind, instead of a small nudge.
Reading Jib Telltales
Streaming straight back
Both telltales flowing aft = well trimmed. This is the state shown on every boat above.
Inner one flutters/lifts
You're pointed too close to the wind (pinching). Fall off a little, or ease the sheet.
Outer one flutters/lifts
Sail is too loose or you've turned too far away from the wind. Trim in, or head up a little.
Telltale = one small piece of yarn/ribbon on each side of the jib, near the front (luff). Reading them is one of your three key questions: "are my sails trimmed properly?" (Note: this is different from the gold wind indicator shown on each boat above, which is a single telltale on the shroud/mast showing your angle to the wind — straight back near irons, straight out to the side near beam reach.)
